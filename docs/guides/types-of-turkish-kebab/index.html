7 types of Turkish kebab to try

A quick guide to Turkish kebabs: döner, iskender, adana, beyti, şiş, köfte and ali nazik, and which ones work best for catering.

By Singapore Kebab. Updated 25 October 2026

In Turkey, kebab doesn't mean one dish. It's a whole family of grilled and roasted meats, and every region has its own. Here are seven you'll meet on Turkish menus, including ours.

1. Döner kebab

Marinated meat stacked on a vertical spit, slow-roasted and carved into thin slices. Served in a wrap, in bread or on a plate with rice. Its history.

2. Iskender kebab

Named after İskender Efendi of Bursa. Döner is laid over cubes of pide bread, then topped with tomato sauce, melted butter and yoghurt on the side. Rich and comforting, and a favourite for sit-down dinners.

3. Adana kebab

From the southern city of Adana. Minced lamb mixed with red pepper is pressed onto a wide iron skewer and grilled over charcoal. It's usually spicier than other kebabs.

4. Beyti kebab

Today's beyti is usually minced meat, grilled, wrapped in thin lavash bread, sliced and served with tomato sauce and yoghurt. The dish is named after Beyti Güler, a well-known Istanbul restaurateur, whose 1960s original used lamb fillet.

5. Şiş kebab

Cubes of marinated chicken or lamb grilled on a skewer. Simple, juicy and easy to share.

6. Köfte

Turkish meatballs or patties, grilled and served with rice, salad and bread. Every region has its own recipe.

7. Ali nazik

Smoky grilled eggplant mashed with yoghurt and garlic, topped with meat. A south-eastern Turkish classic that pairs creamy and savoury.

Which kebabs work best for catering?

  • For live stations: döner, carved to order. It's the most theatrical and the fastest to serve a queue.
  • For office lunches: kebab over rice, wraps and köfte boxes travel well and are easy to eat at a desk.
  • For dinners and weddings: iskender and beyti add a sit-down feel, with meze and baklava to finish.

Quick answers

What is the most popular Turkish kebab?

Döner kebab is the best known worldwide. In Turkey, iskender, adana and şiş kebab are also very popular.

What is the difference between adana and döner kebab?

Adana kebab is spiced minced meat grilled on a wide skewer, while döner is layered meat roasted on a vertical spit and carved into thin slices.
