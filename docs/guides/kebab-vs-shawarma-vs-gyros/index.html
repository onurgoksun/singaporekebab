Kebab vs shawarma vs gyros: what's the difference?

Döner kebab, shawarma and gyros are all carved from a vertical spit. Here's how they differ in origin, meat, sauce and bread.

By Singapore Kebab. Updated 25 October 2026

In Singapore you'll see all three names on menus, sometimes for the same thing. They are related: each is meat roasted on a vertical spit and carved into slices. But they come from different places and taste different.

The short answer

Döner kebab

The Turkish original. The meat is stacked by hand, slow-roasted and shaved off in very thin slices. In Turkey it's served on a plate with rice, in half a loaf of bread, or as a dürüm wrap. Iskender kebab, döner over bread with tomato sauce, butter and yoghurt, is one of its best-known forms. Read the history of döner.

Shawarma

The Arab version, popular from Lebanon to the Gulf. Its name also comes from a Turkish word for "turning". Shawarma marinades often use warm spices, and it's usually wrapped in thin bread with garlic sauce or tahini, pickles and sometimes fries.

Gyros

The Greek version, whose name also means "turn". Gyros is usually pork or chicken, served in a thick pita with tomato, onion, fries and tzatziki.

So which one are you eating in Singapore?

Many shops in Singapore use "kebab" and "shawarma" interchangeably. If the meat is thinly carved from a spit and served with garlic yoghurt in pide or a wrap, it's closest to Turkish döner. If it comes with toum, pickles and fries, it's shawarma style.

Quick answers

Is shawarma the same as kebab?

Not exactly. Both come from a vertical spit, but döner kebab is Turkish and usually carved thinner, while shawarma is the Arab version with its own spices and garlic or tahini sauce.

Is gyros Greek or Turkish?

Gyros is the Greek version of spit-roasted meat. Its name, like döner, means turn.
